# 401(k) Contribution Limits 2025

Source: IRS Publication 560 and 401(k) Plan Rules. Tax year 2025. Federal limits only.

## 2025 401(k) Contribution Limits Summary

For 2025, the annual contribution limit to a 401(k) plan (employee deferrals) is:
- **$24,500** for individuals under age 50
- **$29,500** for individuals age 50 and older (includes $7,500 catch-up contribution)

These limits apply to:
- Traditional 401(k) plans
- Roth 401(k) plans (same employee deferral limits)

**Note:** These are EMPLOYEE deferral limits only. Employer contributions have separate limits (total employee + employer cannot exceed $69,000 or $76,500 if age 50+).

---

## Key Facts About 401(k) Contributions

### Who Can Contribute?
- Must be an employee of a company offering a 401(k) plan
- Must be enrolled in the plan
- Within the annual contribution limits

### Tax Treatment
- **Traditional 401(k)**: Contributions are made pre-tax (reduce current taxable income)
- **Roth 401(k)**: Contributions are made after-tax (grow tax-free)

### Employer Matching
Many employers offer matching contributions:
- Common match: 50% of up to 6% of salary
- Maximum match varies by plan
- Employer matches do NOT count toward your $24,500 individual limit

### Catch-up Contributions (Age 50+)
- Additional $7,500 allowed for individuals age 50 and older
- Total limit becomes $29,500 for age 50+

## Important Differences: 401(k) vs Other Retirement Accounts

### 401(k) vs IRA

| Feature | 401(k) | IRA |
|---------|--------|-----|
| **2025 Contribution Limit** | $24,500 ($29,500 at 50+) | $7,000 ($8,000 at 50+) |
| **Who Can Open** | Must be offered by employer | Anyone with earned income |
| **Employer Match** | Often available | Not available |
| **Loan Provisions** | May allow loans | No loans allowed |
| **Required Minimum Distributions** | Start at age 73 | Start at age 73 |
| **Early Withdrawal Penalty** | 10% penalty before 59½ | 10% penalty before 59½ |
| **Investment Options** | Limited to plan's offerings | Thousands of investment choices |

### 401(k) vs 403(b)

| Feature | 401(k) | 403(b) |
|---------|--------|--------|
| **2025 Limit** | $24,500 | $24,500 |
| **Offered By** | For-profit companies | Non-profit organizations, schools |
| **Employer Match** | Common | Less common |

## How to Maximize 401(k) Contributions

### Step 1: Take Full Employer Match
- Contribute enough to get the full employer match
- This is essentially "free money"
- Example: If employer matches 50% of up to 6%, contribute at least 6% of salary

### Step 2: Contribute to the Max if Possible
- If you can afford it, contribute the full $24,500 (or $29,500 if age 50+)
- Reduces your current taxable income
- Grows tax-deferred (or tax-free for Roth)

### Step 3: Consider Catch-up at Age 50
- If age 50 or older, you can contribute an additional $7,500
- Total becomes $29,500

## 2025 401(k) Contribution Calculation

### Example 1: Single, Under 50
```
Maximum contribution: $24,500 per year
If paid biweekly: $24,500 ÷ 26 = $942.31 per paycheck
If paid monthly: $24,500 ÷ 12 = $2,041.67 per paycheck
```

### Example 2: Married, Age 50+
```
Maximum contribution: $29,500 per year (each spouse)
If paid biweekly: $29,500 ÷ 26 = $1,134.62 per paycheck
If paid monthly: $29,500 ÷ 12 = $2,458.33 per paycheck
```

## Important Limitations

### Earned Income Requirement
- You can only contribute up to 100% of your earned income
- Example: If you earned $15,000, you cannot contribute $24,500

### Plan-Specific Limits
- Your employer's plan may have its own limits
- Some plans may not allow the full IRS limit
- Check with your HR/payroll department for your plan's specific rules

### Annual Deferral Limit Applies to All 401(k) Plans Combined
- If you work for multiple employers with 401(k) plans
- Your total contributions across ALL 401(k)s cannot exceed $24,500
- Example: $12,000 at Job A + $12,500 at Job B = $24,500 total (OK)
- But $15,000 at Job A + $15,000 at Job B = $30,000 total (OVER LIMIT)

## Tax Considerations

### Pre-Tax (Traditional) 401(k)
- Contribution reduces current-year taxable income
- Growth is tax-deferred (no tax until withdrawal)
- Withdrawals in retirement are taxed as ordinary income

### Roth 401(k)
- Contribution does NOT reduce current-year taxable income
- Growth is tax-free
- Qualified withdrawals in retirement are tax-free

### Income Limits
- 401(k) contributions have NO income limits
- Anyone can contribute up to the $24,500 limit
- (Note: Roth IRA has income limits; Roth 401(k) does NOT)

## When to Withdraw from 401(k)

### Allowed Withdrawals (Without Penalty)
- **Age 59½**: Can withdraw without 10% early withdrawal penalty
- **Separation from Service**: Can withdraw at any age if you leave your job
- **Rule 72(t)**: Substantially equal periodic payments (SEPP) at any age
- **Employer Plan Loan**: Some plans allow loans instead of withdrawal

### Early Withdrawal Penalties
- Withdrawal before age 59½: 10% penalty (plus income tax)
- Exception: Penalties may not apply if you separate from service at age 55 or later
- Exception: Roth 401(k) withdrawals of contributions (not earnings) anytime

### Required Minimum Distributions (RMD)
- Start at age 73 (under current law)
- Must withdraw a minimum amount each year
- Failure to take RMD results in 25% penalty on shortfall (reduced to 10-20% under certain conditions)

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Can I contribute more than $24,500?
**A:** You can contribute up to $29,500 if you're age 50 or older. After that, the only additional contribution is employer matching (subject to total limit of $69,000/$76,500).

### Q: What if my employer doesn't offer a 401(k)?
**A:** You can open a Traditional IRA or Roth IRA instead. IRA limits are lower ($7,000 in 2025) but anyone with earned income can contribute.

### Q: Can I contribute to both a 401(k) and an IRA?
**A:** Yes! You can contribute to BOTH. Maximum: $24,500 in 401(k) PLUS $7,000 in IRA (if eligible). However, IRA deductions may be limited if you have a 401(k).

### Q: What if my employer matches 401(k) contributions?
**A:** Employer match does NOT count toward your $24,500 individual limit. Employer contributions have their own limit (combined employee + employer typically limited to ~$69,000 total).

### Q: When do I need to decide: Traditional or Roth 401(k)?
**A:** Usually during open enrollment. Many plans allow you to split contributions between Traditional and Roth.

**Note on Dependents:** The 401(k) contribution limit is based on YOUR earned income and does NOT depend on how many dependents you have. A married couple filing jointly each has their own $24,500 limit ($29,500 if age 50+) at their respective employers.
